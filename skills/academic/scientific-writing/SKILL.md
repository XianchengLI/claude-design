# Scientific Writing

Write scientific manuscripts with precision and clarity. Use IMRAD structure, proper citations, and reporting guidelines for journal submissions.

## When to Use This Skill

- Writing or revising any section of a scientific manuscript
- Structuring a paper using IMRAD or other standard formats
- Improving writing clarity, conciseness, and precision
- Addressing reviewer comments and revising manuscripts
- Preparing manuscripts for submission to specific journals

## Two-Stage Writing Process

**Stage 1: Outline with Key Points**

Create a structured outline:
- Main arguments and findings per paragraph
- Key studies to cite
- Data points and statistics to include
- Logical flow between paragraphs

**Stage 2: Convert to Flowing Prose**

1. Transform bullet points into complete sentences
2. Add transitions (however, moreover, in contrast)
3. Integrate citations naturally within sentences
4. Expand with context that bullet points omit
5. Vary sentence structure for readability

**Final output must be complete paragraphs, never bullet points** (except Methods inclusion/exclusion criteria).

## IMRAD Structure

- **Introduction**: Context, gap, objectives, novelty
- **Methods**: Design, population, procedures, analysis (enough detail for reproducibility)
- **Results**: Findings with statistics, no interpretation
- **Discussion**: Interpretation, comparison with literature, limitations, implications

## Writing Principles

**Clarity**: Precise language, define terms at first use, logical flow, active voice when appropriate.

**Conciseness**: No redundant words, favor shorter sentences (15-20 words avg), respect word limits strictly.

**Accuracy**: Exact values with appropriate precision, consistent terminology, distinguish observation from interpretation.

**Objectivity**: No overstating findings, acknowledge conflicting evidence, neutral tone.

## Section-Specific Guidance

**Abstract**: Standalone summary (100-250 words). Check if journal requires structured (labeled sections) or unstructured format.

**Introduction**: Establish importance, review literature, identify gap, state objectives.

**Methods**: Past tense. Include: study design, participants, procedures, statistical methods with justification, ethical approval.

**Results**: Past tense. Present primary then secondary outcomes. Integrate figures/tables. Report effect sizes alongside p-values.

**Discussion**: Relate to research questions, compare with literature, acknowledge limitations honestly, propose future directions.

## Reporting Guidelines

| Study Type | Guideline |
|------------|-----------|
| Observational | STROBE |
| RCTs | CONSORT |
| Systematic reviews | PRISMA |
| Diagnostic accuracy | STARD |
| Prediction models | TRIPOD |
| Case reports | CARE |

## Common Rejection Reasons

1. Inappropriate or insufficiently described statistics
2. Over-interpretation of results
3. Poor methods reproducibility
4. Small or biased samples
5. Poor writing quality
6. Inadequate literature review
7. Unclear figures/tables
8. Not following reporting guidelines
